What does the cost ratio mean?

The model gives a chance (%) that the outage becomes severe. To turn that chance into a yes / no answer, the app needs a cut-off. The cut-off depends on which mistake is worse:

  • Missed severe outage: the app says "Not severe" but the outage becomes severe, so too few crews are sent.
  • False alarm: the app says "Severe" but the outage stays small, so crews are called for nothing.

3 : 1 means one missed severe outage costs as much as 3 false alarms. The more a miss costs, the lower the cut-off, so the app raises the alarm earlier.

Cost ratioMeaningSays "Severe" when the chance is
1 : 1a miss is as bad as a false alarm50% or more
3 : 1a miss is 3 times worse25% or more
5 : 1a miss is 5 times worse17% or more

Why 25% for 3 : 1? Take an outage with a 25% chance of being severe:

  • If the app says "Not severe": 25% chance it was a miss × cost 3 = 0.75
  • If the app says "Severe": 75% chance it was a false alarm × cost 1 = 0.75

Both choices cost the same, so 25% is the break-even point. Above it, saying "Severe" is cheaper. In general the cut-off is 1 / (1 + c), where c is how many false alarms one miss is worth.

The cost ratio changes only the yes / no answer. The chance (%) itself stays the same. The utility chooses c from its own costs, for example $50,000 for a missed severe outage and $10,000 for a false alarm gives c = 5.
